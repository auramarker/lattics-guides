Knowledge is <Highlight color="Yellow">networked and multidimensional</Highlight>. That is, a certain type of knowledge usually has multiple frameworks and multiple perspectives of understanding. There will also be various connections between different concepts, and these connections constitute a very complex network knowledge base, which is difficult to manage in a hierarchical manner.

To help you build your own <Highlight color="Yellow">networked knowledge base</Highlight>, Lattics offers a variety of methods:

1.  <Highlight color="Yellow">Tag</Highlight>

    <Video src="/images/tag_en.mp4" />

    Tag is a widely used method to associate your knowledge. It requires manual marking. Articles marked with the same tag will be considered to belong to the same category, and even if the articles are not in the same project, they can be retrieved through the tag, thus building cross-project knowledge connections.

    In the Extension Info interface of articles or cards, tags can be marked for them. They can also be marked with the syntax of <Highlight color="Yellow">#tag name</Highlight> in the text.

2.  <Highlight color="Yellow">Citation</Highlight>

    <Video src="/images/cite_en.mp4" />

    Knowledge is often embedded in each other. For example, A concept will involve B concept, and B concept will involve C concept... Just like the entries of Wikipedia, by improving the interconnection between entries, it is easy to construct a huge knowledge base.

    Lattics provides a method of content referencing that allows articles and cards to connect to each other locally instead of online. When you need to cite an article or card, just type <Highlight color="Yellow">@</Highlight> in the text. You can also <Highlight color="Yellow">drag articles or cards</Highlight> from the project outline, card library, or search results into the text.

    There are two citation methods provided by Lattics. One is <Highlight color="Yellow">title citation</Highlight>, which is like a Wiki entry that you can click to view the content. The citation title in the text can be modified at will to suit different writing scenarios. The other is <Highlight color="Yellow">full-text citation</Highlight>, which is very suitable for repeating the full-text content of some famous quotes and poems, and is consistent with the style of the original text. When revising the original text, the full-text citations will also be automatically updated.

    Title citations and full-text citations can be easily <Highlight color="Yellow">converted to each other</Highlight>, just click the Convert button in the lower left corner of the citation pop-up window. Full-text citations can also be turned into a piece of plain text by clicking "Unquote" in the upper right corner of the pop-up window.

    **Note**\: If you need to convert a certain paragraph of text or a certain keyword into a reusable card or entry during the writing process, you can select these words first, and then click "<Highlight color="Yellow">Turn to Card</Highlight>" in the right-click menu. If a certain keyword already has a related entry, a citation to the entry will be automatically created.

3.  <Highlight color="Yellow">Backlinks and unlinked</Highlight>

    If the article or card has been cited, the articles that cited it will be listed in its extension info interface (backlinks), click the link to view and edit the text of the articles.

    Lattics support the "<Highlight color="Yellow">unlinked</Highlight>" feature. It will list all the articles and cards that have mentioned the entry but have not yet cited it, and can establish a citation link with one click, which greatly improves the efficiency of building a networked knowledge base.

4.  <Highlight color="Yellow">Graphs</Highlight>

    <Video src="/images/graph_switch_en.mp4" />

    For a networked knowledge base, the best way to display it is a graph. Lattics provides three very useful graphs:

    1. <Highlight color="Yellow">Mind Map</Highlight>

       Mind maps is great for formulating ideas for writing. When you get started to write something, you can quickly build a project outline through mind map. The title of each node is displayed in the mind map. If you need to edit the text of a node, you can find the editor icon in <Highlight color="Yellow">the right-click menu</Highlight>, or use the shortcut keys <Highlight color="Yellow">Ctrl + E / Command + E</Highlight> to quickly open the editor.

       **Tips**: The node position of the mind map are synchronized with the project outline <Highlight color="Yellow">in same time</Highlight>, that is, if the node of the mind map is adjusted, the project outline will be updated accordingly.

    2. <Highlight color="Yellow">Plot View</Highlight>

       The plot view is great for framing the writing points and main plot of each chapter, and helps in the overall design of the plot development and narrative between chapters. The plot points will be recorded in the memo of the article and will not affect the main text.

    3. <Highlight color="Yellow">Aerial view</Highlight>
       <Video src="/images/nodeconnect_en.mp4" />
       A aerial view is great for revising and overviewing the text. It will display the content of all articles and cards on the graph in abbreviated form, and will also present all the relationships between articles and cards. In the aerial view, you can <Highlight color="Yellow">establish the relationship</Highlight> between two articles by dragging the arrows in the right-click menu, and you can set <Highlight color="Yellow">colors, line types</Highlight>, and mark <Highlight color="Yellow">the relationship name</Highlight> for the connection line.

       In the aerial view, you can drag and drop the lower right corner of each article or card to change their size to facilitate different content display needs.

       **Tips**: Mind Map, Story View, Bird's Eye View all can <Highlight color="Yellow">set a color</Highlight> for each node to mark the importance or priority of the node. You can also remove and delete a node, these functions are in the node's right-click menu.
